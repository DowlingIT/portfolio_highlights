NGS (Next Generation Sequencing) clinical labs move samples through robots, sequencers, and reporting systems that don't talk to each other out of the box. Without automation or software that helps the lab focus on problems, the risk of errors is high - a tough situation for the lab, the physician, and the patient. Complexity is not unique to labs. Any business juggling several systems runs into the same thing.

For a clinical NGS lab running close to a thousand samples a day, I spent two years connecting every major system in the workflow: Hamilton liquid handling robots (file-based and API models), an Illumina sequencer, UV spectrophotometers, notifications by email and fax, and a Salesforce sync for sales and support. All of it running through Mirth Connect, in a HIPAA and CLIA lab.

I also tuned the SQL Server behind it and rebuilt parts of the lab analysis UI, so the system stayed fast and problems stayed visible as volume grew.

I came from the lab, not from software; fifteen years as an analytical chemist before the switch. That background made this integration fit the science, but the skill isn't lab-specific: reading a workflow, finding where manual handoffs create risk, and automating them without losing the audit trail.
